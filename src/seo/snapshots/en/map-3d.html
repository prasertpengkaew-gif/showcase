Web 3D · interactive map

A 3D map your visitors can explore, search and route on

Pick a place, fly to it, see how long the walk is from the station, and what else is within ten minutes. It runs in the browser on any phone or screen — no map app, no install. This one is a sample city; yours uses your own site, venue or project data.

Sample city . Nerd City is made up: the buildings, places, street grid and river are generated for this demo. Routes and times are calculated on its streets (walking 80 m/min, car 30 km/h) — there is no real traffic or transit data.

Where a 3D map helps

  • Property and developments

    Show a project in its neighbourhood: minutes on foot to the station, schools, hospitals and parks, with the buildings and units next to it.

  • Venues, expos and campuses

    Wayfinding for visitors: pick a place, get a route and its time. Works on a phone, on a kiosk or on a big screen.

  • Tourism and cities

    Attractions, hotels and transport on one map, with a day and a night mood, in Thai and English.

  • Industrial estates and campuses

    Plots, gates and routes between buildings, with the machine view of a factory opening from the map (see the Digital Twin demo).

How we would build yours

  1. 1

    Your data

    CAD, BIM or GIS files, OpenStreetMap, drone or survey data, and a simple sheet of places with names, categories and photos.

  2. 2

    Your look

    Brand colours, day and night styles, labels in the languages your visitors use, and your own buildings as detailed 3D models.

  3. 3

    Your features

    Search, filters, routes, walking-time areas, layers, floor-by-floor views of key buildings, and links from a place to your booking or contact page.

  4. 4

    Your delivery

    A web link or QR code, an embed in your site, or a kiosk app. Real maps from map services can be combined where their licence and fees suit the project.

What this demo is not . It does not show a real place, live traffic or public-transport times, and it cannot search real addresses. Those need real data sources and, for some, paid map services.
